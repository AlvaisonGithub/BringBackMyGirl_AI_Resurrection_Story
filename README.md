# Echoes of Her: A Game of Memory, Grief, and AI

An interactive visual novel exploring the ethical questions surrounding AI resurrection: should we recreate someone we have lost through artificial intelligence, and what happens to their autonomy, identity, and memory when we do?

Created by **Alva Chen**, a Computational Social Science student at the **University of Amsterdam**.

## About the Game

You step into the life of a father navigating work, family, illness, grief, and the possibility of digitally recreating his deceased daughter. Through branching conversations and consequential decisions, the story asks players to balance emotional connection with respect for personal autonomy and consent.

Rather than offering a definitive answer, *Echoes of Her* invites players to reflect on the ethical and emotional costs of using AI to preserve—or simulate—the presence of someone who has died.

## Key Features

- **Branching narrative:** Player choices influence conversations and the direction of the story.
- **Two evolving measures:** `HeartWarmth` reflects emotional connection, while `AgencyCredits` tracks respect for autonomy.
- **An AI resurrection dilemma:** Players consider whether to create an AI representation of the deceased daughter, including questions of consent and the impact on surviving family members.
- **Multiple possible story outcomes:** Decisions shape the experience and its closing reflections.
- **Ethical reflection:** The game draws on the ethics of care and deontological ethics, including empathy, responsibility, autonomy, truth, and consent.
- **Easter egg:** The game has ONE easter egg, which is the storyline that now even the creator cannot achieve anymore.

## Ethical Questions

- Can an AI replica provide comfort without misleading those who are grieving?
- Can someone meaningfully consent to their digital recreation after death?
- Who should have a say in creating or interacting with a digital representation of a deceased person?
- Does recreating a person's voice or personality preserve their memory, or risk replacing it with a simulation?

## Academic Context and References

This project was developed in the context of Computational Social Science at the University of Amsterdam. Its ethical framing is informed by work on AI-based resurrection, digital afterlife, grief, and the moral implications of simulating deceased people.

Selected references presented in the game include:

- Heder, M. (2021). *AI and the resurrection of technological determinism*. Információs Társadalom, 21(2), 119.
- Lu, H. (2024). *Exploring the predictors of public acceptance of artificial intelligence-based resurrection technologies*. Technology in Society, 78, 102657.
- Rodriguez Resendiz, H., & Rodriguez Resendiz, J. (2024). *Digital resurrection: Challenging the boundary between life and death with artificial intelligence*. Philosophies, 9(3), Article 71.
- Turchin, A. (2019). *You only live twice: A computer simulation of the past could be used for technological resurrection*.
- Rohrer, J. (2023). *Project December: Simulate the Dead*.

Additional references are available in the game's **About → References** section.
